How binary works

Our numbers use ten digits, 0–9. Binary uses only two — 0 and 1 — like a light switch: off or on. Every computer stores everything as those two states.

Each position is worth double the one to its right — 1, 2, 4, 8, 16… A position showing a 1 is counted; one showing a 0 is skipped. So 1010 means 8 + 2 = 10.
